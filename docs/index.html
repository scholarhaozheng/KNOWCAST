01 / THE IDEA

Beyond knowing where.
Understand the way.

Fare gates tell us where passengers enter and leave. They do not tell us which paths they take. KNOWCAST connects demand forecasting and route assignment inside one end-to-end learning framework.

Transportation theory supplies the structure. Deep learning learns how that structure adapts to observed demand. Trip generation, trip distribution, and traffic assignment shape the features, training objective, and architecture of a shared forecasting process.

Network-level OD forecasts and inferred path flows remain directly linked through an embedded differentiable assignment mechanism, without requiring path-level ground-truth labels.

DEMAND RECONSTRUCTION

Complete the demand picture

AFC records distinguish completed journeys, ongoing journeys, and the actual OD target. Fixed, training-period destination rankings retain dominant destinations for each origin and collect the remainder in one residual column. Day, week, and month references help allocate ongoing trips without using unobserved same-day destinations.

Three GATRUs and an attention gate encode unfinished-trip allocations. A parallel GATRU processes completed demand. The residual preserves total origin demand.

LOSS GUIDANCE

Guide the early learning

An independent DCRNN estimates station-level production and attraction using catchment demographics, temporal attributes, and event indicators. A knowledge-informed loss encourages the predicted OD marginals to agree with these estimates.

The loss weight decays as 0.0264 × max(1 − 0.1 × epoch, 0), so this guidance acts during the first 10 epochs. Its clearest benefit is early convergence, with limited change in final accuracy.

DISTRIBUTION FEATURES

Encode where demand goes

A doubly constrained gravity model turns station production, attraction, and dynamic travel impedance into coarse OD proportions. Iterative proportional fitting balances the matrix at each time interval. An autoencoder compresses the result into forecasting features.

The gravity deterrence coefficient is calibrated before main training and held fixed. The feature encoder is pretrained for reconstruction and then fine-tuned with the forecaster.

DIFFERENTIABLE ASSIGNMENT

Make assignment learnable

Candidate-path attributes enter a linear utility function. A Logit layer normalizes the utilities into route-choice probabilities for each OD pair. The station-count and transfer coefficients start from transportation priors and can adapt through the OD forecasting objective.

An OD–path–section tripartite graph links each route to the directed sections it traverses. The learned coefficients are forecasting-calibrated operational parameters, rather than universal behavioral estimates.

NETWORK-AWARE FEATURES

Represent the service network

Section availability, path traversal probabilities, and departure frequencies encode changing network operations. Separate autoencoders create compact path-choice and service-frequency features for the main predictor.

COMPONENT 5.1

Section passage probability

Candidate routes and section availability determine how likely an OD journey is to traverse each directed network section. This produces a path-aware feature before forecasting.

COMPONENT 5.2

Departure frequency influence

Time-varying train service changes the attractiveness of feasible paths. Its frequency feature is encoded separately before joining the OD predictor.

Line-level service embeddings are computed once per time step and shared across OD pairs. Unavailable sections have zero service. Future inputs must be known, announced, or forecastable at the prediction origin.

Inside KITP: architecture & information flow §4.1–4.8

A graph-attention recurrent learner

Historical OD features are combined with distributional, path-choice, and service-frequency augmentations. Graph attention weights feasible neighbors under a fixed adjacency mask; gated recurrence captures temporal dependence.

A forecast-origin information boundary

The encoder uses only realized history. The decoder starts from zero OD input and the encoder state. Training uses teacher forcing; inference feeds back its own predictions. Future unfinished-trip branches are removed.

Seven input groups

AFC demand; trip-generation explanatory features; static gravity preferences; dynamic impedance and station totals; utility priors; time-varying path attributes; and dynamic service-network structure. Scheduled future conditions must be available at the forecast origin.

07 / CONCLUSIONS & SCOPE

A unified approach.
With clear boundaries.

Transportation mechanisms can supply useful inductive structure for deep learning, while keeping OD forecasts and path-flow estimates connected throughout the model.

What this study establishes

  • Better OD prediction than the ten tested baselines on the City S dataset.
  • An end-to-end framework that couples forecasting with assignment-based path inference.
  • Observable adaptation of knowledge-initialized parameters during training.
  • Indirect evidence that section estimates align with observed train-load patterns.

What remains open

  • Cross-city and newly opened line generalization were not tested.
  • AFC supplies no realized route labels; inferred paths remain structurally constrained estimates.
  • Residual demand has no unique path assignment; compression changes the prediction target.
  • Future work includes initialization sensitivity, nonlinear interactions between integrators, and emergency or disruption resilience.

Abstract, authors & publication details ARTICLE INFORMATION

Abstract

In metro operations, Origin-Destination (OD) flows and path flows are critical for transportation planning, but their accurate short-term forecasting and estimation remain challenging due to the complex influencing factors that affect passenger behavior. In addition, due to data collection challenges, previous research has generally prioritized short-term OD flow forecasting over path flow estimation. To address these challenges, this paper presents a domain-knowledge-integrated framework for jointly forecasting short-term network-level OD flows and inferring path flows, with path-flow inference directly coupled to the predicted OD flows through an embedded assignment mechanism.

The framework systematically integrates transportation domain knowledge of trip generation, trip distribution, and traffic assignment into a sequence-to-sequence architecture. Knowledge-based components and their jointly optimized intermediate path representations couple path-flow inference to OD forecasting. Domain-informed parameter initialization supplies a starting point for adaptive learning; parameter trajectories show how the network refines those priors.
